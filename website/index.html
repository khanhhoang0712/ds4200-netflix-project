Netflix Content Strategy Evolution (2016–2021)

DS4200 Information Presentation and Data Visualization  ·  Khanh Hoang, Alma Abubakari, Kittirat Trakulsujaritchok

Overall Introduction

This project analyzes how Netflix's content strategy changed between 2016 and 2021. As Netflix expanded from a more limited streaming library into a global platform, its catalog shifted in terms of content type, budget, ratings, and international reach. The goal of this project is to use data analysis and visualization to better understand how Netflix's strategy evolved over time and what trends stand out most clearly in the dataset. This topic is important because Netflix is one of the most influential streaming platforms in the world, and changes in its content strategy reflect broader shifts in media production, audience demand, and global entertainment markets. Prior research on film release timing strategies and the growth of streaming content catalogs provides useful context for understanding the patterns we observe in Netflix's data.

Introduction to the Data

Our analysis uses the "Netflix – Exploratory Data Analysis" dataset from Kaggle, which covers all content released on Netflix from 2008 to 2021. The dataset contains over 8,000 rows and 12 columns, including a mix of categorical variables such as content type (Movie or TV Show) and country of origin, as well as numerical variables like release year and duration (in minutes or seasons). To enrich our analysis, we also merged in financial metadata from a supplementary Kaggle dataset (movies_metadata.csv), which provided production budget information for movies. Additionally, we focused our analysis on 2016 to 2021, the period of most significant strategic change for Netflix, and created a new rating_group column by parsing and grouping the original rating codes into broader audience categories (Mature, Teen, Older Kids, Kids, and Other).

Data Visualizations

1. Geographic Distribution of Netflix Content

This interactive world map shows the geographic distribution of Netflix content by country of origin. Each country is shaded in red, with darker colors indicating a higher number of titles produced in that country. Use the dropdown menu to filter the map by content type — All Titles, Movies Only, or TV Shows Only. Hover over any country to see its name and the exact number of Netflix titles it has produced.

Takeaway: The map reveals a clear concentration of Netflix content in a small number of countries, with the United States dominating by a significant margin. India and the United Kingdom are the next largest contributors, followed by Canada, Japan, and South Korea. This distribution makes sense from a business perspective because the US is Netflix's largest and oldest market, and the platform naturally invested most heavily in content that would appeal to its core subscriber base first. India and the UK also represent Netflix's two largest international markets by subscriber count, which explains the high volume of content from those countries. The strong presence of South Korean and Japanese content reflects the massive global demand for K-dramas and anime, suggesting that Netflix has strategically leaned into these markets as a way to attract subscribers across Asia and beyond.

2. Netflix Content Growth and Type Evolution (2016–2021)

This side-by-side line chart shows two related trends. The left chart tracks the total number of titles added to Netflix each year from 2016 to 2021. The right chart shows how the share of Movies versus TV Shows shifted over the same period. Hover over any point to see exact values, and click on a content type in the legend to highlight or fade that line.

Takeaway: Netflix's catalog grew rapidly from 2016 through 2019, peaking at over 2,000 titles added in a single year before declining in 2020 and 2021, likely due to COVID-19 disruptions and a broader strategic shift toward quality over quantity. At the same time, movies consistently made up the larger share of content — roughly 60 to 75% each year — though their dominance gradually declined as the share of TV shows slowly increased. This suggests Netflix was not only scaling back total output but also rebalancing its catalog toward more episodic content, which tends to drive stronger viewer engagement and longer time spent on the platform.

3. Netflix Genre Rankings Over Time (2016–2021)

This bump chart tracks how the top 8 genres on Netflix were ranked each year from 2016 to 2021, based on the number of titles added. Each colored line represents one genre, and its vertical position shows whether it ranked higher or lower that year compared to others. Click on any genre in the legend to highlight its trend and fade the others.

Takeaway: International Movies and Dramas consistently ranked at the top throughout the period, reflecting Netflix's strong focus on globally appealing content. However, there were notable shifts: Documentaries rose in ranking around 2017 and 2018 before falling back, suggesting a temporary spike in demand for non-fiction content. Independent Movies declined steadily in rank over time, which may reflect a shift away from smaller productions toward bigger budget titles. Overall, the genre rankings show that Netflix's content strategy was not static but it continuously adjusted which types of content it prioritized in response to audience demand and competitive pressures.

4. Distribution of Movie Budgets on Netflix (2016–2021)

This layered chart combines a boxplot with an average trend line to show how movie production budgets were distributed across years from 2016 to 2021. Each boxplot shows the spread and variance of budgets for that year, while the yellow line tracks the average budget over time. Hover over any element to see exact budget values.

Takeaway: Average movie budgets on Netflix were relatively low and consistent between 2016 and 2018, then increased noticeably starting in 2019. The boxplots also reveal that budget variance grew significantly after 2018, Netflix was not just spending more on average, but also taking on a wider range of productions from lower budget indie films to high budget blockbusters. This pattern supports the broader story of Netflix shifting from a strategy of adding large volumes of affordable content to one that includes more premium, high-investment productions alongside a continued presence of independent films.

5. Netflix Content Rating Distribution by Year (2016–2021)

This heatmap shows how the share of each content rating group changed across years from 2016 to 2021. Rows represent rating categories ordered from youngest to most mature audiences, and the color intensity shows the percentage of content in each group for that year. Each cell also displays the exact percentage. Hover over any cell for more details.

Takeaway: Mature-rated content has consistently dominated Netflix's catalog every year, accounting for roughly 40 to 47% of all titles added between 2016 and 2021. Teen-rated content is the second largest group at around 30%, while Kids and Older Kids content together remain below 30% combined. The proportions stayed remarkably stable over the entire period, which suggests this is a deliberate and sustained content strategy rather than a shifting trend. Netflix clearly prioritizes adult audiences as its core demographic, which also differentiates it from more family-oriented competitors like Disney+.

Summary and Additional Work

From these visualizations, several key conclusions emerge about Netflix's content strategy from 2016 to 2021. First, Netflix expanded its catalog aggressively through 2019 before pulling back, shifting from a volume-first approach to a more selective, quality-focused strategy. Second, while movies consistently dominated the catalog, the gradual rise of TV shows suggests Netflix recognized the value of episodic content for retaining subscribers. Third, genre rankings shifted over time — International Movies and Dramas remained dominant, but fluctuations in genres like Documentaries and Independent Movies reveal that Netflix continuously adjusted its priorities in response to audience demand. Fourth, average movie budgets increased significantly after 2018 and variance grew, indicating Netflix was investing in a wider range of productions including more premium content. Finally, mature-rated content remained the dominant category throughout, confirming that Netflix's core audience targeting strategy remained consistent even as other aspects of its catalog evolved.

For future work, distinguishing between Netflix original content and licensed content would provide insights into how Netflix's investment in originals has changed over time. Adding viewership data would also allow for a more complete analysis of which content strategies actually resonated with audiences. There are also some limitations worth noting. The dataset only covers content from 2016 to 2021 and does not capture more recent developments such as password sharing restrictions, the introduction of an ad-supported tier, and intensifying competition. Additionally, the financial budget data was only available for a subset of movies, which means the budget analysis may not fully represent the entire catalog.
