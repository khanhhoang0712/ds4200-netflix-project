Netflix Content Strategy Evolution (2016–2021)

DS4200 Information Presentation and Data Visualization  ·  Khanh Hoang, Alma Abubakari, Kittirat Trakulsujaritchok

Overall Introduction

This project analyzes how Netflix's content strategy changed between 2016 and 2021. As Netflix expanded from a more limited streaming library into a global platform, its catalog shifted in terms of content type, budget, ratings, and international reach. The goal of this project is to use data analysis and visualization to better understand how Netflix's strategy evolved over time and what trends stand out most clearly in the dataset. This topic is important because Netflix is one of the most influential streaming platforms in the world, and changes in its content strategy reflect broader shifts in media production, audience demand, and global entertainment markets. Prior research on film release timing strategies and the growth of streaming content catalogs provides useful context for understanding the patterns we observe in Netflix's data.

Introduction to the Data

Our analysis uses the "Netflix – Exploratory Data Analysis" dataset from Kaggle, which covers all content released on Netflix from 2008 to 2021. The dataset contains over 8,000 rows and 12 columns, including a mix of categorical variables such as content type (Movie or TV Show) and country of origin, as well as numerical variables like release year and duration (in minutes or seasons). To enrich our analysis, we also merged in financial metadata from a supplementary Kaggle dataset (movies_metadata.csv), which provided production budget information for movies. Additionally, we focused our analysis on 2016 to 2021, the period of most significant strategic change for Netflix, and created a new rating_group column by parsing and grouping the original rating codes into broader audience categories (Mature, Teen, Older Kids, Kids, and Other).

Data Visualizations

1. Netflix Catalog Growth Over Time (2016–2021)

This line chart shows how the total number of titles added to Netflix changed from 2016 to 2021. Hover over any point on the line to view the exact number of titles added that year and follow the overall growth trend across time.

Takeaway: Netflix's catalog grew rapidly from 2016 through 2019, reaching its peak around 2019 with the highest number of titles added in a single year. After that, the number of new titles began to decline in 2020 and 2021. This slowdown is likely caused by a few factors: the impact of COVID-19 on global content production, and perhaps a broader strategic shift away from quantity toward fewer but higher quality productions. This pattern suggests that Netflix's early growth strategy was focused on rapidly expanding its library, before transitioning into a more selective approach as competition from other platforms intensified.

2. Movies vs. TV Shows Added Per Year (2016–2021)

This grouped bar chart compares the percentage of Movies and TV Shows added to Netflix each year from 2016 to 2021. Each year has two bars, allowing viewers to compare the share of movies versus TV shows added that year. Hover over any bar to see the exact percentage and count. You can also click on a category in the legend to highlight or hide that content type.

Takeaway: Movies made up the larger share of Netflix content every year between 2016 and 2021, consistently making up roughly 60-75% of all titles added. However, the movie share peaked around 2018 and gradually declined after that, and the share of TV shows increased slightly. This shift indicates a change in consumer preference and perhaps a strategic change, as TV shows tend to drive stronger viewer engagement through longer formats which encourages subscribers to spend more time on the platform. This suggests that while Netflix still prioritizes movies more, its strategy is slowly shifting towards growing its shows catalog to improve customer retention.

3. Average Movie Budget Over Time (2016–2021)

This bar chart shows the average production budget of movies added to Netflix each year from 2016 to 2021. The x-axis shows the year and the y-axis represents the average movie budget in millions of dollars. Hover over any bar to see the exact average budget and the number of movies that were used to calculate that average for that year.

Takeaway: Average movie budgets on Netflix were relatively lower between 2016 and 2018, then increased noticeably starting in 2019. There is a slight dip in 2020 likely due to COVID-19 disrupting production schedules and financing. But overall, the average budget remained significantly higher than in the earlier years and rose again in 2021. This trend supports what we observed in the catalog growth chart: as the total number of titles added began to decline after 2019, the average budget per movie increased, indicating that Netflix deliberately shifted its strategy away from adding large volumes of content toward investing in fewer but higher-budget and more premium productions.

4. Content Rating Distribution by Year (2016–2021)

This grouped bar chart shows how Netflix content ratings were distributed across years from 2016 to 2021. Each color represents a different rating category. Hover over any bar to see the exact count and percentage, and use the dropdown menu to filter by a specific rating group.

Takeaway: Mature-rated content has consistently dominated Netflix's catalog every year, accounting for roughly 40-47% of all titles added between 2016 and 2021. Teen-rated content is the second largest group, making up around 30% of titles, while Kids and Older Kids content together remain below 30% combined across all years. The proportions stayed stable over the entire period, which suggests this is a deliberate and sustained content strategy. Netflix clearly prioritizes adult audiences as its core demographic, which also differentiates it from more family-oriented competitors like Disney+.

5. Geographic Distribution of Netflix Content (D3)

This interactive world map shows the geographic distribution of Netflix content by country of origin. Each country is shaded in red, with darker colors indicating a higher number of titles produced in that country. Use the dropdown menu to filter the map by content type — All Titles, Movies Only, or TV Shows Only. Hover over any country to see its name and the exact number of Netflix titles it has produced.

Takeaway: The map reveals a clear concentration of Netflix content in a small number of countries, with the United States dominating by a significant margin. India and the United Kingdom are the next largest contributors, followed by Canada, Japan, and South Korea. This distribution makes sense from a business perspective because the US is Netflix's largest and oldest market, and the platform naturally invested most heavily in content that would appeal to its core subscriber base first. India and the UK also represent Netflix's two largest international markets by subscriber count, which explains the high volume of content from those countries. The strong presence of South Korean and Japanese content reflects the massive global demand for K-dramas and anime, suggesting that Netflix has strategically leaned into these markets as a way to attract subscribers across Asia and beyond.

Summary and Additional Work

From these visualizations, one of the main conclusions is that Netflix expanded its catalog very quickly between 2016 and 2019, suggesting a strong period of aggressive growth before slowing down after 2019, likely influenced by COVID-19 and increasing competition. A second major conclusion is that while movies consistently made up the larger share of Netflix content, TV shows grew in importance over time, suggesting that Netflix is broadening its approach to retain subscribers through longer content. A third key finding is that average movie budgets increased in the later years while mature-rated content consistently dominated the catalog, which shows that Netflix was not only adding more content but investing more heavily in premium content targeted at adult audiences. Finally, the geographic distribution of content shows that Netflix's catalog remains heavily concentrated in a small number of markets — particularly the United States, India, and the United Kingdom — which reflects where its largest subscriber bases are located.

For future work, distinguishing between Netflix original content and licensed content would provide insights into how Netflix's investment in originals has changed over time. Rather than just measuring what was added to the catalog, adding viewership data would also allow for a more complete analysis of which content strategies actually resonated with audiences. There are also some limitations to this analysis worth noting. The dataset only covers content from 2016 to 2021, which is a relatively short window and does not fully capture how Netflix's strategy has continued to evolve in response to more recent challenges such as password sharing restrictions, the introduction of a new ad-supported subscription tier, and intensifying competition. Additionally, the financial budget data was only available for a subset of movies, which means the budget analysis may not fully represent the entire catalog.
